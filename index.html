01 About

I completed a combined B.S. and M.S. in Data Science at Worcester Polytechnic Institute in May 2026. Between an internship, undergraduate research, and graduate project work, my attention has kept returning to the same question: what it actually takes to move a model out of a notebook and into something an organization can depend on.

Python anchors the technical side — PyTorch, TensorFlow, and Scikit-learn for model development and deployment, with Pandas and NumPy handling preparation upstream. I also work in SQL, across cloud platforms such as Snowflake and Azure, and in Tableau for reporting, and I increasingly rely on LLM-based tooling for automation, prompt design, and extracting structure from unstructured sources.

That toolkit has been pointed at fairly different problems: enterprise data engineering and workflow automation, applied deep learning research, forecasting in noisy domains like equity markets and medical imaging, and deployment under the tight constraints of embedded hardware.

Throughout, I benchmark competing options — architectures, prompting strategies, optimization methods — before committing to one, and I treat the system surrounding a model as no less important than the model itself. The work I find most compelling sits in the gap between a prototype that runs and a system that stays reliable.
